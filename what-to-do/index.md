## For personal situations

Slow down the moment that feels frictionless. Ask what was delivered, what was meant, and what you inferred. Request the missing context instead of guessing. When an AI system is involved, check the source, name the uncertainty, and keep a human relationship from being reduced to an automated reply.

Add your own practical example here. The strongest advice will be specific enough to use tomorrow: a question to ask, a pause to take, or a boundary to set.

## For professional situations

Make the communication system inspectable. Document who sets the goal, who encodes the message, who is expected to decode it, and who can challenge the preferred reading. Test outputs with people who occupy different positions. Build a route for correction that does not require the affected person to become an expert.

## A compact practice

1. Name the channel and the noise.
2. Notice the signs and cultural assumptions.
3. Ask who controls the preferred meaning.
4. Separate author, work, reader, and algorithmic intention.
5. Choose a response that gives people more context and more agency.
