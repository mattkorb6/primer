## A message is made of signs

Words, interfaces, images, voices, and pauses do not carry one universal meaning. They become meaningful through conventions and contrasts. A chatbot's friendly greeting, for example, can signal care, salesmanship, or an uncanny imitation depending on the reader and the setting.

Describe the signs in your chosen problem. What is literal? What is implied? Which cultural habits make the interpretation feel natural?

## The culture around the signal

Semiotics adds the surrounding world: the codes people share, the differences they notice, and the stories that make a sign legible. An AI output is never only generated text. It appears inside a product, a workplace, a language, and a moment with its own expectations.

This is the place to examine context. Whose cultural references are assumed? Which meanings travel well, and which are flattened or misunderstood? What does the phenomenon ask its audience to take for granted?

## Questions to carry forward

- Which signs do people interpret differently?
- What cultural code makes the interpretation possible?
- What meanings become less visible when a system standardizes language?
