## Three intentions in tension

Eco's model distinguishes intentio auctoris (the author's intention), intentio operis (the work's internal possibilities), and intentio lectoris (the reader's interpretation). The distinction prevents us from treating one person's explanation as the only meaning available.

Use the three intentions to map your phenomenon. What did the human author or institution want? What does the message or interface permit on its own terms? What does the reader bring to it?

## Naming the algorithm

This site proposes *intentio algorithmi* as a working name for the patterned pressure introduced by an algorithmic system: ranking, prediction, optimization, and learned association. It is not a human mind hiding inside the machine. It is a way to name what classical author-text-reader diagrams could not fully name: a system that shapes the field of possible readings without being a conventional author.

Treat this as a claim to test, not a conclusion to repeat. In your analysis, show what the term reveals and where it risks giving a system too much agency. Who chose the objective? Who benefits from the ranking? What gets made likely before the reader arrives?

## Questions to carry forward

- Which intention is being confused with another?
- What patterned pressure does the algorithm add?
- What human decisions remain behind the apparent machine intention?
