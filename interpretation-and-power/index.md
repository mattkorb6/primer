## Preferred readings are designed

Encoding is not just putting words into a container. It is the selection of a frame, a vocabulary, an image of the audience, and a version of what counts as normal. Decode the message in your case by asking who designed its preferred reading and what that reading asks people to accept.

With an AI system, encoding can be distributed across training data, product choices, prompts, policies, and the person who deploys the tool. Name those layers rather than treating the output as ownerless.

## Audiences have room to move

Hall's dominant, negotiated, and oppositional readings offer a practical vocabulary for disagreement. A reader may accept the intended meaning, adapt it to local experience, or reject the frame entirely. None of these readings happens outside social position.

Apply these positions to your example. Who can afford to negotiate? Who has the authority to reject an output? Whose interpretation is treated as a mistake instead of evidence?

## Questions to carry forward

- Who has the power to encode the preferred reading?
- Which audience is imagined, and which audience is missing?
- What would an oppositional reading make newly visible?
